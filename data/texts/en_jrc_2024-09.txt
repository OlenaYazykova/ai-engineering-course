Severe drought led to below-average
yield outlook for summer crops

Throughout the summer of 2024, Ukraine experienced
unusually dry conditions. While yields of winter and spring
crops remained close to the 5 -year average, the outlook
for summer crops is below average, but without reaching
a historical low.
Spring weather conditions were generally favourable in
most of the country. However, as of June, temperatures
consistently exceeded the long -term average , and an
intense drought affected two -thirds of the country from
late June to early September. These drought condition s
had limited impacts on winter crop yields, as the season
was about to end. However, they severely affected
summer crops in all oblasts east of Kyyivs'ka.
The non- government controlled areas experienced a
significant increase in abandoned land, leading to a
substantial decrease in cultivated areas compared to last
year. In the rest of the country, the total area of arable
land was preserved. Wheat, barley, rapeseed, grain maize,
and sunflower areas decreased compared to 2023, while
the soybean area continued its positive trend.
The production of wheat and barley is significantly below
the 5-year average and 2023, primarily due to reduced
harvest areas. Both grain maize and sunflower production
suffered from reduced yields and areas, whereas soybean
production is expected to increase due to the expansion of
sown areas. The production in Russian-occupied territories
was substantially reduced due to the increase in
abandoned land and the strong yield reduction. We
estimate that of the total production of summer crops, 1%
of grain maize, 13% of sunflowers, and 2% of soybeans
remain in non-government controlled areas.

Meteorological overview
Distinctly warmer -than-usual conditions prevailed in all of Ukraine during the summer months. Marked positive
temperature anomalies characterised northern and western regions in the first half of July and the second half of August,
and intensified in early September. Whereas a rainfall surplus was observed mainly in northern central regions in June
and parts of western regions in early July, much drier- than-usual conditions developed and persisted in central and
eastern regions in July, and in most of Ukraine in August.

The weather synthesis map represents a summary of the most distinct weather anomalies during the reporting period
(1 June to 10 September 2024) compared with the 1991– 2023 long -term average (LTA) for the same period.
Temperature surplus and rainfall deficit are unusual absolute and relative deviations from the LTA, considering the
reporting period as a whole. Hot spells are 5-day periods with temperatures above the 90th percentile of the years since
1991. The single-indicator maps below provide further context on each type of event.
June
• Warmer-than-usual conditions, with average daily temperatures between 1 °C and 2 °C above the LTA in almost all
regions and between 2 °C and 3 °C above the LTA in a region extending across the border between Kirovohrads’ka
and Dnipropetrovs’ka, southernmost Odes’ka and easternmost Luhans’ka1.
• Wetter-than-usual conditions in western regions and central northern regions, with cumulative precipitation mostly
between 100 % and 150 % above the LTA; more than 150 % above the LTA in Chernihivs’ka and locally in Sums’ka.
