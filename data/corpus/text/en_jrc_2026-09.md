# JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook: Crop monitoring in Ukraine, September 2026

<!-- page 1 -->

Joint Research Centre

Issued: 21 September 2026, JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

## JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

Crop monitoring in Ukraine – September 2026

## Fair summer crop yields expected, except in the west

Mixed weather conditions prevailed during the summer, leading to a contrasting yield outlook for summer crops. Persistent drought in the west and rainfall deficits in parts of the centre resulted in below-average yields, while fair to positive yields are expected elsewhere, supported by timely water supply and the absence of severe thermal stress.

For winter crops, the generally drier- and warmer-than-usual weather in June did not significantly change crop conditions, matching the overall positive yield outlook reported in the June edition of this bulletin. However, rain in July delayed harvesting and deteriorated crop quality.

Following a delayed sowing campaign for summer crops due to locally dry and cold weather in spring, a reduction in soybean area was observed. In the west and parts of the centre, crops delayed in development faced rainfall shortages together with above-average temperatures, resulting in suboptimal vegetative growth. Elsewhere, crop conditions remained fair to positive, mainly due to abundant rainfall in July. The impact of rain deficit in August is expected to be limited.

Considering the spatial distribution of production areas, slightly above-average yields are forecast for grain maize and sunflower, while, for soybean, mainly grown in the west, yields are expected to be below average at the country level. Because of this and the slightly increased areas of grain maize and sunflower, the production for both grain maize and sunflower is expected to be fair, while soybean production is expected to fall well below the five-year average.

## 1. Weather review (1 June – 10 September 2026)

Summer was characterised by marked regional variability in temperature, with warmer-than-average conditions in the west and cooler conditions concentrated in the east, particularly in July. Rainfall was generally below average in June, especially in the east, while July was notably wetter than usual across eastern regions. August was drier than usual, again across most regions.

## 2. Crop growth conditions

The map displays the relative differences (in percentages) between the cumulative fraction of absorbed photosynthetically active radiation (fAPAR) from 1 August to 10 September 2026 and the medium-term average (MTA) (2016–2025) for the same period. Positive anomalies (in green) reflect above-average crop biomass, while negative anomalies (in red) reflect below-average biomass or late crop development.

The August vegetation anomaly map shows the heterogeneous crop conditions across the country. Vegetation growth was generally above the medium-term average (MTA) in the northern and north-eastern regions, notably in Chernihivs’ka, Sums’ka, Poltavas’ka and parts of Kharkivs’ka and Luhans’ka, indicating favourable conditions for summer crops. In contrast, below-average biomass accumulation was widespread in the south, particularly across Khersons’ka, Mykolayivs’ka, Zaporiz’ka, Donests’ka, and northern Odes’ka, with pronounced negative anomalies locally, reflecting the combined effects of irrigation system failures following the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam in 2023 and the expansion of abandoned agricultural land. Central regions show a mixed pattern, with alternating areas of above- and below-average vegetation growth. In the west, the fAPAR was generally below the MTA, in line with the reported drought. Overall, the map suggests that the recovery of summer crops from delayed sowing was uneven and insufficient to fully offset the effects of the delay and spring water stress, particularly in the west and in parts of the central production areas.

## 2.1 Winter and spring crop growth conditions

During grain filling and the ripening of winter cereals, the absence of significant thermal stress during the reporting period helped to maintain an overall positive yield outlook. The harvest campaign began late and was further delayed by abundant and frequent rainfall in July and early August, reducing crop quality locally. Harvest reports confirm that the above-average wheat yield forecast was accurate in the government-controlled oblasts. The near-average rapeseed yield outlook was close to the reported harvests too, while the forecast for barley was slightly below the harvest reports.

Winter and spring cereals

<!-- page 9 -->

In June, no considerable heat stress occurred in Ukraine; daily maximum temperatures hardly exceeded 30 °C in the north of the country, and typically fewer than 10 hot days (Tmax > 30 °C) were registered in the south. The absence of thermal stress provided adequate conditions for grain filling in most of Ukraine. In the west (e.g. L’vivs’ka, Volyns’ka, parts of Rivnens’ka and Ternopil’s’ka), however, winter and spring cereals kept suffering from water stress due to the persistent drought. Rain in early June arrived late, in particular for winter cereals, and just moderately improved the yield expectations. Biomass accumulation remained at below-average levels in these regions, as confirmed by crop model simulations. The effect of the prolonged water stress is also clearly visible in cropspecific indicators derived from satellite imagery for wheat and barley. In the east, soil moisture decreased to below-average levels in late June (e.g. in Luhans’ka and Donets’ka), but the impact on winter and spring cereals was limited, since they were at the end of the grain-filling stage. Winter cereals’ leaf area index (LAI) and biomass accumulation were generally adequate until the end of the grain-filling period, with a promising yield formation. The harvest campaign of winter cereals started later than usual, following the delayed crop development during the growing season. Cropspecific LAI profiles generally present 10–20 days of delay in development, except in some western oblasts (e.g. Ternopil’s’ka, Vinnyts’ka). Harvest started in early July and accelerated only after mid July. Abundant rainfall caused the temporal suspension of fieldwork in the west (L’vivs’ka, Ivano- Frankivs’ka, Khmel'nyts'ka), north (Sums’ka, Kharkivs’ka, Luhans’ka) and centre (Dnipropetrovs’ka, Poltavs’ka) during July and early August, increasing the risk of deteriorated grain quality. Overall, the yield forecasts show a positive outlook for soft wheat and barley.

Rapeseed

Late cold spells in April resulted in an interrupted vegetative phase and canopy expansion, particularly in the west, but photosynthetic activity generally reached above-average levels in late May or early June thanks to favourable conditions in late spring and the considerable recovering capacity of rapeseed, especially during flowering. During the grain-filling period, soil moisture conditions were favourable in the most important rapeseedproducing oblasts but remained poor in the west and in parts of the centre. In June, some rainfall arrived in western regions, partially alleviating the crop water stress, but grain formation remained constrained. In the south west, by contrast, moderate temperatures and fair soil moisture supply supported yield formation, maintaining a positive outlook for yield potential (e.g. in Vinnyts’ka and Odes’ka). As a result, the overall yield outlook at the national level remained fair despite adverse weather in some regions, confirming the assessment reported in the June edition of the bulletin. The harvest of rapeseed started later than usual, in the first dekad of July. Frequent and abundant precipitation led to further delays, with no significant harvest progress until 20 July. The wet conditions may have reduced seed quality and caused some harvest losses. According to the harvest reports of the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food, the harvest was completed by early September.

## 2.2 Summer crop growth conditions

The summer crop season was marked by a cold and relatively wet sowing campaign, resulting in delayed fieldwork, with an increased share of late-sown fields. Seasonal or slightly above-average temperatures in most parts during summer prevented the acceleration of crop development until late July. As a result of this, the persistent drought in the west and a rainfall deficit in parts of the centre and south-east, crop development remained mixed across Ukraine. Grain maize and sunflower yields are forecast slightly above the five-year average, resulting in above-average production, taking an increased grain maize area into account. Below-average yields are forecast for soybean, and well-below-average production is expected, given the reduced production area.

Grain maize

The season started slowly in late April, as cold spells significantly delayed sowing in several parts of the country. Fieldwork accelerated during the second half of May, but a high share of late-sown fields remained. Nevertheless, the total sown area at the country level slightly exceeded the five-year average, according to the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food. In the western oblasts, including Volyns’ka, L’vivs’ka, Ternopils’ka and Rivnens’ka, the delayed onset of the season had been compounded by persistent drought since early spring and above-average, although not extreme, temperatures. These conditions resulted in poor crop development, with the maize-specific leaf area index (LAI) indicating near-record to record-low vegetative development. Well-below-average yields are expected in this region.

Delayed phenology was also observed in parts of the central and northern oblasts, including Chernihivs’ka, Poltavs’ka and Kirovohrads’ka. However, abundant rainfall in July, particularly across the east of the country, combined with the absence of severe thermal stress, supported an improvement in crop conditions. Vegetative development progressed well after late June, although conditions remained heterogeneous across regions. Grain maize entered the flowering stage under fair to favourable conditions, particularly in Vinnyts’ka, Dnipropetrovs’ka, Kharkivs’ka, Luhans’ka and most of Kyivs’ka. The rainfall deficit in August is expected to have affected grain filling, particularly in the central oblasts. Below-average yields are anticipated in the centre, notably in Kirovohrads’ka and Cherkas’ka, while the outlook is more favourable in the northern oblasts, including Poltavs’ka and Sums’ka. Overall, the country-level yield forecast remains slightly above the five-year average, pointing to

Sunflower near-average production, despite the negative outlook in the west and the mixed conditions in the central oblasts.

The Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food indicated a slightly above-average sunflower area for the 2026 crop season. As with grain maize, the season started slowly: late cold spells significantly delayed sowing in several parts of the country. Fieldwork accelerated during the second half of May, but there was still a high share of late-sown fields, resulting in delayed crop development in most regions. Sunflower nevertheless benefited from its deeper rooting system, which helped it withstand the early-season moisture stress better than grain maize. Crop growth generally started in the second dekad of June and was supported by above-average temperatures until late June, allowing the crop to partially catch up after its delayed onset. In the western oblasts, including Volyns’ka, L’vivs’ka and Rivnens’ka, the persistent drought with no or little relief throughout the summer provided the most unfavourable conditions. Well-below-average yields are forecast, although the impact is expected to be less severe than for grain maize. In neighbouring oblasts, including Khmelnyts’ka and Zhytomyrs’ka, conditions were heterogeneous but remained generally fair thanks to above-average rainfall in June. Abundant rainfall in July, particularly across the east of the country, supported the recovery of crops. Vegetative development improved as crops progressed into the reproductive stages, particularly in Kharkivs’ka, Dnipropetrovs’ka, Luhans’ka and, further south, Odes’ka. Fair to above-average yields are expected in these areas. Given the high concentration of sunflower in the southern and eastern oblasts, where the crop outlook is generally favourable, the country-level outlook remains slightly above the five-year average, hence offsetting the disappointing yields anticipated in the west and in parts of the centre.

<!-- page 15 -->

Soybean

The season started slowly, as late cold spells significantly delayed or even hindered sowing in several parts of the country. Given the persistent dryness and late cold spells during spring across much of the soybean-producing area, the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food reported a reduction in the sown area, estimated at 25% below the area of the previous season. Fieldwork accelerated during the second half of May, but a high share of late-sown fields remained, resulting in delayed crop development in most regions. Soybean was particularly exposed to the adverse conditions in the west and centre, where a large share of the national production is concentrated. In the western oblasts, including Rivnens’ka, L’vivs’ka and Volyns’ka, persistent drought led to severely deteriorated soybean conditions in the major producing areas. The soybean crop-specific LAI indicated well-below-average biomass accumulation throughout the season, with record-low vegetative growth observed in these oblasts. The neighbouring oblasts, including Zhytomyrs’ka and Ternopils’ka, were also significantly affected. More favourable vegetation patterns were observed in Vinnyts’ka and in parts of Poltavs’ka and Sums’ka. As the worst-affected areas this season account for a large share of national soybean production, the impact on the country-level outlook is particularly pronounced. The yield forecast is below the five-year average, making soybean the most affected of the three summer crops analysed in this edition of the bulletin. This, together with the reduced area, means that production is expected to fall by around 23% compared with the five-year average.
