# JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook: Crop monitoring in Ukraine, September 2025

<!-- page 1 -->

Issued: 15 September 2025

JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

Ukraine, September 2025

## JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook Crop monitoring European neighbourhood Ukraine

## September 2025 Contrasting yield outlooks for summer crops

Ukraine experienced unusually dry conditions in summer in the southern and eastern oblasts, leading to well belowaverage yields for summer crops there. In contrast, an adequate water supply and the absence of severe heat stress supported a positive yield outlook in the northern and western regions, despite delayed development. A dry start to the summer of 2025 negatively affected the final stages of winter crop development in the central oblasts. In the west, the harvesting campaign was hampered by excessive rainfall in July, which reduced the quality of both grains and rapeseed. Altogether, winter crop yield expectations were slightly below average. While production forecasts for wheat and rapeseed are above the five-year average, thanks to an increased sown area, barley production is expected to fall below average, as the area has been considerably reduced. During most of summer, a rainfall deficit persisted in the eastern and southern oblasts, negatively affecting grain maize, sunflower and soybean. By contrast, the northern and western oblasts profited from an adequate soil moisture, maintaining summer crop yield potential despite delayed development from late sowing and emergence.

Yield forecasts for Ukraine - 15 September 2025 Bulletin

At the national level, grain maize and soybean yields are forecast to be above the five-year average. Soybean production has been further supported by an expansion in cultivated area, while a limited reduction in maize area has not been sufficient to offset the expected yield gains, resulting in production estimates that are still above the five-year average. Sunflower yields, however, are expected to remain below average, largely due to the drought in the south, where most sunflower cultivation takes place; this prediction is supported by the oblast-level analysis. However, the production outlook remains around average, as the sown area increased.

## Weather Review

Distinctly drier-than-usual conditions prevailed across southern and eastern Ukraine during the summer months, while excessively wet conditions characterised the western regions in July. Positive thermal anomalies were observed in June and July in most regions, followed by below-average temperatures in August, except in the southern oblasts. Warmer and drier than usual conditions are forecast until late September.

The weather synthesis map summarises the most distinct anomalies during the reporting period (1 june – 9 September) compared with the 1991–2024 long-term average (LTA). Rain deficit and surplus are absolute and relative deviations from the LTA.

## June

Slightly warmer-than-usual conditions, with average daily temperatures between 0.5 °C and 1 °C above the LTA, in the south and west, but slightly colder than usual (average daily temperatures up to 1 °C below the LTA) in central northern regions and north-eastern regions; near-average conditions elsewhere.

Wetter-than-usual conditions in the northernmost oblasts, with cumulative rainfall mostly between 50% and 100% (locally up to 150%) above the LTA in northern Chernihivs’ka and Sums’ka. Drier than usual, with cumulative rainfall between 50% and 100% below the LTA, in most of the rest of Ukraine.

## July

Warmer-than-usual conditions in most of the country, with average daily temperatures between 0.5 °C and 2 °C above the LTA, especially in the east and south-east.

Significantly wetter-than-usual conditions in the west, with total rainfall up to 150%, locally more, above the LTA in northern L’vivs’ka and southern Volyns’ka, and also in most of Khmel’nyts’ka, Zhytomyrs’ka and neighbouring oblasts. Less distinct rainfall surpluses in parts of north-eastern oblasts (eastern Poltavs’ka, south-western Kharkivs’ka), with total rainfall between 50% and 100% above the LTA. Contrasting drier-than-usual conditions in most eastern and south-eastern regions, with total rainfall between 50% and 100% below the LTA.

<!-- page 4 -->

## August

Slightly colder than usual in most northern and central oblasts, with average daily temperatures between 0.5 °C and 1 °C below the LTA, and near-average temperature conditions in southern and easternmost parts.

Drier-than-usual conditions prevailed in most of Ukraine, with total rainfall between 50% and 100% below the LTA in the regions of Volyns’ka and Rivnens’ka in the northwest, and also in Vinnyts’ka and neighbouring oblasts, in most of Odes’ka and in some eastern and south-eastern oblasts (Dnipropetrovs’ka, Zaporiz’ka, Khersons’ka, Krym). Average rainfall in the north and northeast.

## September

Observed weather, 1–9 September

Warmer-than-usual start to autumn throughout the country, with average daily temperatures between 0.5 °C and 2 °C above the LTA in most eastern regions and central northern regions, and more distinct anomalies westwards, with temperatures reaching up to 6 °C above the LTA in the westernmost oblasts.

Dry conditions, with total rainfall between 50% and 100% below the LTA, prevailed in most oblasts, except in Krym, Khersons’ka and neighbouring areas; isolated downpours in Kyyivs’ka, Khmel’nyts’ka and Zakarpats’ka.

<!-- page 5 -->

Weather forecast, 10–19 September

Warmer-than-usual conditions are forecast for most of Ukraine, with average daily temperatures between 0.5 °C and 2 °C above the LTA in most of the eastern and some of the southern oblasts, between 2 °C and 3 °C in the western oblasts and parts of the northern oblasts and locally up to 4 °C in Vinnyts’ka and neighbouring areas. Nearaverage to slightly below-average temperatures are forecast for Krym and southern Khersons’ka.

Drier-than-usual conditions, with rainfall deficits up to 100% of the LTA, are forecast for large parts of Ukraine. In contrast, from Khmel’nits’ka westward, and in parts of Krym, cumulative rainfall is expected to exceed the LTA by up to 100% or more. The most distinct positive anomalies are forecast for Volyns’ka, Ivano-Frankivs’ka, Zakarpats’ka and eastern Krym.

## Crop growth conditions

## Winter and spring crops

Favourable temperatures and the absence of considerable thermal stress during the grain-filling and ripening periods maintained a positive yield outlook for winter and spring cereals in western and northern Ukraine. However, significantly drier-than-usual conditions in June negatively affected the yield formation of winter crops in the central oblasts and reinforced our below-average yield expectations in southern and south-eastern Ukraine. The harvest campaign, currently nearing completion, started on time but was delayed by abundant and frequent rainfall in July, particularly in western parts of the country, and this also led to reduced crop quality. Our updated yield forecasts confirm a fair outlook for cereals but point to below-average rapeseed yields at the national level. Recent area estimates have confirmed an increase in the area under winter crops compared with the previous campaign, supporting the overall winter crop production of the country.

Daily maximum temperatures exceeded 30 °C only for a few days in June. The absence of severe thermal stress generally supported a fair completion of the winter and spring cereals season, especially in the western and northern oblasts, where adequate soil moisture allowed crops to remain in good condition. Crop model simulations and crop-specific Sentinel-2 data (see Appendix B) confirm above-average biomass accumulation in these regions, maintaining the positive yield outlook reported in the June edition of this bulletin (1).

By contrast, the rainfall deficit in some southern oblasts (Odes’ka, Mykolayivs’ka) and most central oblasts (Cherkas’ka, Kirovohrads’ka, Poltavs’ka) depleted the soil moisture content and negatively affected the grain filling of cereals, resulting in reduced yield expectations. Rapeseed, which had already suffered from a late cold spell in spring during the flowering and early pod-filling stages, was similarly impacted, resulting in a further deterioration in its yield potential at the national level. The harvest campaign for winter crops started on time at the end of June and progressed smoothly through early July before being delayed for several weeks due the abundant and frequent rainfall from mid July onward, especially in the central and western oblasts (L’vivs’ka, Volyns’ka, Khmel’nits’ka, Zhytomirs’ka). The wet conditions not only hampered fieldwork but also reduced grain quality. According to the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food (MAPF2), the harvest is nearly complete. Our updated remote-sensing-based area analysis (see Appendix A) confirms the higher rapeseed acreage reported in June, particularly in the southern oblasts (Odes’ka, Mykolayivs’ka), and points to slightly higher wheat and barley areas than our initial June estimates suggested. This finding is in line with the harvest reports at the oblast level from the Ukrainian MAPF. Our area estimates and yield forecasts at the oblast level confirm the above-average wheat and rapeseed production and the disappointing barley season at the national level.

## Summer crops

The summer crop season was marked by a cold and wet sowing campaign, resulting in delayed emergence and development throughout the season, particularly in the western oblasts. Nevertheless, the generally favourable weather conditions during summer meant that fair yield potential was maintained in this main producing area. By contrast, parts of the central oblasts, together with southern and eastern Ukraine, faced continued drier-than-usual conditions, owing to which yield prospects deteriorated. In the eastern oblasts, crop failure may even have occurred. Altogether, our remote-sensing-based area estimates and our yield forecasts point to fair national summer crop production this year, despite the disappointing sunflower yield outlook, particularly in the south.

The summer crop sowing campaign was hampered by a cold spell and abundant precipitation in early April and May and was completed in early June, with a delay of up to three weeks in sowing on average. The cool and wet spring led to slower crop emergence and early development, particularly in the western oblasts (Ternopil’s’ka, Khmel’nyts’ka, Zhytomyrs’ka). Rainfall in July ensured an adequate soil moisture supply, while August remained drier than usual. Given the nearseasonal temperatures throughout summer, grain maize, sunflower and soybean grown in western Ukraine remain delayed throughout the season, explaining the aboveaverage fAPAR signal currently observed, when a declining signal of ripening crops might usually be expected. In the central oblasts and in the north, the situation is more mixed. Delays in crop development were less pronounced and generally made up thanks to above-average temperatures in July. In the north (Chernihivs’ka, Sums’ka), a rainy June followed by near-seasonal weather later in the summer supported strong biomass accumulation. By contrast, the central oblasts (Kirovohrad’ska, Poltavs’ka) faced unfavourable conditions, with an insufficient soil moisture supply due to the overall drier-than-usual summer and two heatwaves in early and late July, leading locally to deteriorations in crop condition, with belowaverage biomass production. In the southern oblasts (Odes’ka, Mykolayivs’ka, etc.) and eastern oblasts (Dnipropetrovs’ka, Zaporiz’ka, etc.), summer crops were severely affected by persistent drought throughout the season, compromising crop and canopy development. Remote sensing images reveal significantly reduced biomass accumulation compared with previous years. Crop-specific Sentinel-2 data (see Appendix C) for grain maize, sunflower and soybean show the lowest fAPAR signal since 2019. In this part of Ukraine, crop failure may have occurred locally, and yields are forecast to be well below the five-year average. During their final reproductive stages, summer crops benefited from cooler-than-usual conditions in August across most of Ukraine. This supported fair yield expectations in western parts, although the delayed phenology limited yield potential. In the north, the cooler weather further enhanced yield prospects. By contrast, it did not bring any relief to the central oblasts, where yields are projected to fall below average, or to the southern and eastern oblasts, where yields are expected to be far below average. Our yield outlook at the national level predicts aboveaverage yields for grain maize and soybean, largely produced in the central, northern and western regions, while below-average yields are forecast for sunflower, which is cultivated particularly in the central and southern oblasts. The generally drier- and warmer-than-usual weather in September allowed a timely start to the soybean and sunflower harvesting campaigns in the southern and eastern oblasts, as reported by the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food 3, albeit with reduced expectations. Only a few fields have been harvested so far in the west. Here, grain maize has not reached maturity yet, but, with above-average temperatures forecast, harvesting is expected to begin before mid-September.

## Appendices: Crop area and crop phenology – an analysis using Copernicus Sentinel-2 data Appendix A: Crop area estimates

The area estimates for winter and summer crops were derived from a crop type map produced by the Kyiv Polytechnic Institute. The methodology is based on Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 data, following the process presented in the June edition of this bulletin4, updated with data to 20 August. Given the known confusion between winter and spring cereal classes, we first computed the total area of cereals (winter and spring) through pixel counting at the oblast level. This area was then split using sowing and harvest reports from the Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food. Specifically:

- wheat and barley were separated using the shares reported in the harvest data at the oblast level;

- spring and winter barley were further split based on autumn and spring sowing reports at the oblast level;

- rapeseed area was taken directly from the crop type map. For summer crops, pixel counting was applied at the oblast level to the three monitored crops, namely grain maize, sunflower and soybean. As already observed of the 2024 crop type map, the method tends to overestimate the area. To correct for this, we used 2024 pixel-counting data and consolidated official area statistics (on governmentcontrolled oblasts only) to derive three crop-specific correction factors, which were then applied at the oblast level to the 2025 pixel-counting results.

## Appendix B: fAPAR crop-specific analysis

We first derived the fAPAR profiles for all parcels before aggregating them using the crop type map described in Appendix A. This allowed to assess crop-specific development by comparing aggregated profiles with parcels represented on the same crop type from preceding years. The analysis was carried out both at the oblast level (as detailed in the crop growth conditions section) and on a 10 km grid (Figure 1). By leveraging high-resolution Sentinel-2 imagery, this approach provides a substantial improvement to the method normally employed in the bulletin, which relies on non-crop-specific MODIS data at lower spatial resolution.
