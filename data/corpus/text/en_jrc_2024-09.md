# JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook: Crop monitoring in Ukraine, September 2024

<!-- page 1 -->

Issued: 17 September 2024

JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

Ukraine, September 2024

## JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook Crop monitoring European neighbourhood Ukraine September 2024 Severe drought led to below-average yield outlook for summer crops

Throughout the summer of 2024, Ukraine experienced unusually dry conditions. While yields of winter and spring crops remained close to the 5-year average, the outlook for summer crops is below average, but without reaching a historical low.

Spring weather conditions were generally favourable in most of the country. However, as of June, temperatures consistently exceeded the long-term average, and an intense drought affected two-thirds of the country from late June to early September. These drought conditions had limited impacts on winter crop yields, as the season was about to end. However, they severely affected summer crops in all oblasts east of Kyyivs'ka. The non-government controlled areas experienced a significant increase in abandoned land, leading to a substantial decrease in cultivated areas compared to last year. In the rest of the country, the total area of arable land was preserved. Wheat, barley, rapeseed, grain maize, and sunflower areas decreased compared to 2023, while the soybean area continued its positive trend.

The production of wheat and barley is significantly below the 5-year average and 2023, primarily due to reduced harvest areas. Both grain maize and sunflower production suffered from reduced yields and areas, whereas soybean production is expected to increase due to the expansion of sown areas. The production in Russian-occupied territories was substantially reduced due to the increase in abandoned land and the strong yield reduction. We estimate that of the total production of summer crops, 1% of grain maize, 13% of sunflowers, and 2% of soybeans remain in non-government controlled areas.

## Meteorological overview

Distinctly warmer-than-usual conditions prevailed in all of Ukraine during the summer months. Marked positive temperature anomalies characterised northern and western regions in the first half of July and the second half of August, and intensified in early September. Whereas a rainfall surplus was observed mainly in northern central regions in June and parts of western regions in early July, much drier-than-usual conditions developed and persisted in central and eastern regions in July, and in most of Ukraine in August.

The weather synthesis map represents a summary of the most distinct weather anomalies during the reporting period (1 June to 10 September 2024) compared with the 1991–2023 long-term average (LTA) for the same period. Temperature surplus and rainfall deficit are unusual absolute and relative deviations from the LTA, considering the reporting period as a whole. Hot spells are 5-day periods with temperatures above the 90th percentile of the years since 1991. The single-indicator maps below provide further context on each type of event.

## Weather forecast 11–20 September

- With the exception of Zakarpats’ka, where near-average daily temperatures are forecast, distinct positive temperature anomalies relative to the LTA are forecast for most regions, from up to 2 °C above the LTA in southern Odes’ka and south-western regions (Chernivets’ka, Ivano-Frankivs’ka, L’vivs’ka, Ternopil’s’ka, southern Khmel’nyts’ka) to 6 °C or more in north-eastern regions (Chernihivs’ka, Sums’ka, Kharkivs’ka, Luhans’ka).

- Dry conditions (cumulative precipitation of up to 3 mm) are forecast for eastern regions (Sums’ka, Kharkivs’ka, Luhans’ka, Donets’ka, Zaporiz’ka, and parts of Khersons’ka and Krym). Wet conditions (cumulative precipitation of more than 90 mm) are forecast for southern and south-western regions (Ivano-frankivs’ka, Chernivets’ka, Ternopil’s’ka, Khmel’nyts’ka, Vinnytis’ka and Odes’ka). Cumulative precipitation of more than 100 mm is forecast in the regions bordering Moldova and in parts of Khmel’nyts’ka, Ternopil’s’ka and Ivano-frankivs’ka.

## Crop growth conditions Winter and spring crops

Warmer-than-usual weather resulted in accelerated crop development and led to early harvests. In the eastern oblasts, pronounced soil moisture deficits and heatwaves negatively affected soft wheat during the grain-filling period. Weather conditions in the central and western oblasts were more favourable for yield formation. Yield forecasts for winter crops were maintained. The forecast for spring barley was revised slightly downwards.

As reported in the June edition of this bulletin, significant precipitation at the beginning of June mitigated any negative impacts of the prevailing water deficit in most of Ukraine, particularly in the north, but was insufficient to benefit winter crops in the east, where heatwaves and continued inadequate water supply caused severe losses to the yield potential. This year, winter wheat, winter barley and rapeseed presented exceptionally accelerated phenological development; therefore, the harvest started around mid June in the southernmost regions. In the last dekad of June and in July, dry and hot weather in the central and eastern regions provided favourable conditions for harvesting. In the western regions (e.g. Rivnens’ka, Khmelnyts'ka and Ternopil's'ka), harvesting was hampered by periods of abundant rainfall. However, overall progress of harvesting was fast, and it was practically concluded by 22 August, with low harvest losses and little negative impact on grain quality. We maintain our yield forecasts for winter crops as published in the June edition of this bulletin. Meanwhile, we have updated the area statistics in accordance with the harvest report of the Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food of Ukraine (instead of the sowing report). Consequently, the area and production figures reported here are different from the figures reported in June. Spring barley benefited from early sowing thanks to mild spring temperatures. Favourable conditions continued to prevail in the western and central regions, whereas in the eastern production regions, the inadequate water supply and hot temperatures compromised the yield outlook. On the balance, our spring barley yield forecast is slightly above the 5-year average. The harvest of spring barley started in late June and progressed well except during the rainy periods in the west. The harvest was completed in late August.

## Summer crops

Differences between the fraction of absorbed photosynthetically active radiation (fAPAR) cumulated from 1 to 10 September 2024 and the medium-term average (MTA, 2014-2023) for the same periods, in arable land areas.

Spring weather conditions were generally favourable in two thirds of the country, with the exception of the eastern part, where rainfall deficits persisted from March onwards. Beginning in June, temperatures persistently surpassed the LTA, and an intense drought affected most of the country from late June to early September, significantly affecting the outlook for summer crops.

Grain maize sowing occurred at a normal pace, and was concluded by the end of May. Favourable weather conditions prevailed during the early development stage in western and most central oblasts. However, in the eastern half of the country (including Poltavs'ka and Cherkas'ka), the intensifying rainfall deficit had a strong impact on maize, particularly in the south-east

(Khersons’ka and Zaporiz'ka). Conversely, in western and northern areas (e.g. Chernihivs'ka), adequate water supply up to mid August created favourable conditions for grain maize. Although the persistently positive temperature anomaly was detrimental to the overall yield potential, the absence of extreme maximum temperatures (they rarely exceeded 36 °C) mitigated the temperature's impact on yields. Grain maize harvesting commenced earlier than usual, especially in drought-affected parcels in southern oblasts. Sunflower production in Ukraine occurs predominantly in the eastern and south-eastern regions, which were severely affected by the extremely dry conditions prevailing in July and August. Among the major sunflowerproducing oblasts, only Vinnyts'ka (in the central west) has received rainfall close to the LTA since June. The drought had a profound impact on sunflower crops, which are cultivated without irrigation systems in Ukraine. As a result, harvesting began earlier than usual in the eastern regions, driven by accelerated phenology. Soybeans is mostly cultivated in the western half of the country, which was less affected by the drought. The higher-than-normal temperatures were overall negative for crop development. The rain deficit in August in almost all of the country brought forward the senescence phase and shortened the grain-filling phase. It is worth noting that, in the oblast of Khersons’ka, historically a big producer of soybean under irrigation, the drought was also associated with irrigation failure, certainly due to the destruction of the Kakhovka dam in 2023. At the national level, the harvest has started earlier than usual, and field reports sustain an above-average outlook, except in southern and eastern oblasts. Our yield forecast at the national level for the three summer crops is below the 5-year average for grain maize (– 8%) and sunflowers (– 7%), and just above the 5-year average for soybean (+ 1%). This translates to decreases in yields compared with the yields observed in 2023, by 17% for grain maize, 9% for sunflowers and 9% for soybean.

## Appendix A- Soft wheat and rapeseed forecasts evaluation

A comparison (Figure 1) was made between the June 2024 forecast for soft wheat and rapeseed reported in the JRC MARS Bulletin and the figures of the final harvest reports provided by the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food (MAPF). For soft wheat, at the national level, the June JRC MARS forecast for soft wheat was 0.33 t/ha (6.5%) lower than the yield derived from the August harvest report. For rapeseed, the discrepancy was limited to –0.02 t/ha (0.8%).

## B- Monitoring of abandoned land

Based on the 2023 and 2024 crop type maps produced by the Kyiv Polytechnic Institute (KPI), abandoned land was identified and aggregated at the village level (Figure 2). Approximately 4.8 Mha of agricultural land was abandoned in 2024, according to the crop classification map provided by KPI, which represents an increase of

## 0.3 Mha compared with 2023. As in 2023, abandoned land

in 2024 remains largely concentrated in and along the conflict zone, where land abandonment has intensified. This intensification is particularly pronounced in nongovernment-controlled areas, and in eastern parts of Dnipropetrovs’ka and Kharkivs’ka. In other areas, the increase in abandoned land from 2023 to 2024 has been limited; and in some areas there has been a decrease in abandoned land (e.g. Odes’ka, western Khersons’ka), suggesting that Ukrainian farmers are making concerted efforts to maintain grain production.

## C- LAI crop-specific analysis

Leaf area index (LAI) profiles for land parcels with summer crops were derived from Copernicus Sentinel-2 remote sensing data, utilising the Kyiv Polytechnic Institute’s

2019–2024 crop classification map (Figure 3). Although the 2024 map, processed in July, is not final, a comparison with ground samples has demonstrated its high level of accuracy. The growth patterns of summer crops were assessed by drawing comparisons with patterns in parcels from the same oblast in crop maps from previous years. The LAI for parcels with the three monitored summer crops (grain maize, sunflower and soybean) were aggregated at the regional level to provide a comprehensive overview. This approach uses highresolution Sentinel-2 imagery, providing a significant improvement with respect to the standard method employed in the Bulletin, which relies on non-crop-specific

MODIS data. This analysis yields more accurate remote sensing data, which enhances the accuracy of yield predictions at the oblast level.
