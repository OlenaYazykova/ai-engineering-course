# JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook: Crop monitoring in Ukraine, April 2023

JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

## JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook Crop monitoring European neighbourhood

## Ukraine April 2023 Weather conditions permit fair yield outlook

Mild temperatures combined with sufficient rainfall since the start of the 2022-2023 season currently maintain a fair-to-positive yield outlook for winter crops in a major part of Ukraine. However, winter cereal production is forecast significantly below the 5-year average in both government-controlled and non-controlled areas due to reduced sown areas.

A decrease in winter crop sown areas due to the lower availability of labour and agricultural input because of Russia’s war against Ukraine is noted. This does not only apply for the non-government controlled areas, but also in the oblasts under the control of Ukraine. While rapeseed sowing progressed rapidly in August and September 2022, the exceptionally wet conditions during autumn resulted in a slow start to the winter cereal sowing campaign. Nevertheless, the mild temperature allowed favourable germination and early development prior to entering winter. Since then, the unusually warmer-thanusual temperatures throughout the winter and the early spring have allowed winter crops to pass the dormancy period without any detrimental frost damage and to restart their growth under good conditions. More rainfall is needed in the eastern and, especially, southern parts of the country to allow soil moisture conditions to be restored and to avoid future yield losses.

The JRC MARS yield forecasts for winter crops currently follow historical trends. In terms of production, this translates into a forecast of 25.48 Mt of wheat, 3.77 Mt of winter barley and 3.04 Mt of rapeseed, of which 20.67 Mt (wheat), 3.08 Mt (winter barley) and 2.71 Mt (rapeseed) are forecast for the government-controlled areas. These initial forecasts will be revised depending on areas estimate adjustments and on updated MARS yield forecasts during the remaining season.

## Meteorological overview

To date, in most parts of the country, the winter crop season has been characterised by an exceptionally wet autumn with close-to-average temperatures, followed by an unusually mild winter with close-to-average rainfall. The beginning of spring provided significantly above-average temperatures in most oblasts, with a distinct rain deficit in the southernmost regions.

## Autumn (September, October, November) Exceptionally wet autumn with close-to-average temperatures

- Near-seasonal temperatures and wetter-than-usual conditions prevailed in most of the country during autumn. Temperatures in the southernmost and easternmost oblasts were predominantly slightly above the 1991-2021 long-term average (LTA). Rainfall was particularly abundant in the north-eastern oblasts (e.g. Luhans’ka, Kharkivs’ka), where precipitation reached up to twice the LTA. Odes’ka was the only oblast with below-average rainfall.

<!-- page 3 -->

- In September, slightly below-average temperatures prevailed. Rainfall was abundant and frequent in most oblasts, most distinctly in the western oblasts (e.g. Lvivs’ka, Rivnens’ka). Below-average rainfall (up to 50% below the LTA) occurred only in parts of the Khersons’ka and Zaporiz’ka oblasts.

- In October, abundant precipitation continued in the northern and eastern oblasts, while a rain deficit was observed in the southern oblasts (e.g. Odes’ka, Vinnyts’ka). Temperatures were up to 2 °C above the LTA in central and eastern parts, and up to 4°C above the LTA in the west.

- In November, abundant precipitation prevailed throughout the country, except in the westernmost oblasts where rainfall was up to 50% below the LTA. Temperatures remained up to 2°C above the LTA in most regions.

## Winter (December, January, February) Unusually mild winter with close-to-average rainfall

- Ukraine experienced an exceptionally mild winter, most distinctly in the central and western oblasts where mean daily temperatures exceeded the LTA by up to 4 °C.

- In most parts of the country, winter precipitation was average to slightly below average. A distinct rainfall surplus, with precipitation up to 50% above the LTA, was observed in the westernmost and northernmost oblasts (e.g. Chernihivs’ka, Rivnes’ka). A distinct rainfall deficit was recorded in some southern and eastern oblasts (Odesk’a, Donets’ka), where precipitation was up to 50% below the LTA.

- January was particularly dry and warm for most of the country. In the westernmost oblasts, temperatures were up to 6 °C above the LTA.

- Near-seasonal precipitation prevailed in February, except in the southern and easternmost oblasts where significantly drier-than-usual conditions persisted. Above-average temperatures persisted until the end of the month.

## March Distinct rain deficit in the southernmost regions and mild conditions overall

- Drier-than-usual conditions prevailed throughout most of the country during the first two dekads of March. Precipitation was up to 50% below the LTA in most regions, but more distinct rain deficits (up to 80%) occurred in parts of the southern and western oblasts (e.g. Odes’ka, Vinnyts’ka).

- Substantial rainfall was observed over a large part of the country, while below-average precipitation continued in the south-eastern oblasts during the final days of March.

- Temperatures until 20 March remained -2-4°C above the LTA in most regions, and the mild conditions continued until the end of the month.

## Crop growth conditions

The rapeseed sowing campaign progressed rapidly, while soft wheat and winter barley sowing was delayed due to wetter-than-usual autumn conditions. However, the predominantly mild temperatures provided favourable conditions for germination and early development. The continued mild temperatures during winter and early spring resulted in the absence of any signicant detrimental frost damage across the country and in the early restart of vegetative growth. Hence, winter crops are in fair-to-good condition and our yield forecasts follow historical trends.

The map displays the differences between the Normalised Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) cumulated from 1March to 20 March 2023 and the medium-term average (MTA, 2013-2022) for the same period in arable land areas. Positive anomalies (in green) reflect above-average canopy density or early crop development, while negative anomalies (in red) reflect below-average biomass accumulation or late crop development.

The 2022 rapeseed sowing started in the second dekad of August. Sowing progressed rapidly, with 98% of the sowing completed before 20September, and the final fields completed in the first dekad of October. The oblasts of Dnipropetrovs’ka, Khmelnyts’ka, Vinnyts’ka, Ternopils’ka and Odes’ka are the major rapeseedproducing regions. The sowing campaign for winter wheat and winter barley started slowly during the first dekad of September due to the abundant and frequent rainfall, but progressed well in the last two dekads of October. The mild autumn temperatures allowed sowing to continue until the end of November. According to our estimate, a reduction of 7% compared with the 5-year average in the areas with winter crops is observed in the government-controlled areas. This is likely due to the possible lack of agricultural input and labour to perform the fieldwork. In the non-government-controlled areas, our remotesensing analysis (see appendix) suggests that winter crops were sown and grown during autumn 2022 in the oblasts of Zaporiz’ka, Khersons’ka, Donets’ka and Luhans’ka. Our analysis suggests that the area of winter crops in these regions decreased by 22% compared with the 2021-22 season. Despite the delayed sowing, the wet and mild autumn in the main winter-crop growing regions provided a positive start to the season. Crops were overall sufficiently developed prior to entering winter dormancy, thanks to optimal soil moisture conditions in most regions, except in Odes’ka. The persistent warmer-than-usual conditions throughout the winter prevented winter crops from being exposed to any severe cold spells. Temperatures rarely dropped below −12°C. Hence, our crop model did not signal any detrimental frost damage and shows that winter crops lost most of their frost tolerance quite early compared with the previous season. Our crop model shows that winter crops broke their dormancy period and restarted their vegetative growth from the beginning of spring, while maintaining a fair-topositive yield potential if favourable weather conditions prevail during the rest of spring. Rainfall is needed in the eastern and, especially, southern parts of the country to allow the soil moisture conditions to be restored and to avoid yield losses.

<!-- page 6 -->

This picture is also confirmed by our remote sensing analysis. In the western parts of the country, the vegetation index indicates an increase in post-winter canopy density compared with the MTA, which is attributed to the favourable weather conditions during autumn and winter. However, in the southernmost part of the country, notably in Odes’ka, south of Mykolayivs’ka, and Crimea, canopy density appears to be below the MTA, most likely due to the persistent rain deficit1. Our yield forecasts for winter crops currently follow historical trends. In the eastern half of the country, we used the 5-year average to attempt to take into account the sub-optimal management practices (machinery, fertilisers, labour, etc.) since the beginning of Russia’s war against Ukraine. In the western half of the country, we assume more stable conditions and, consequently, used a linear trend. Moreover, the dry and warm conditions in March resulted in an early start to the sowing campaign for spring and summer crops. According to the Ukrainian Ministry of Agriculture 2, sowing started early compared with last season and is currently being carried out in all the government-controlled oblasts.

## Appendix

## Estimation of winter crop area trends using high-resolution remote sensing analysis

We have continued to build on the analysis of highresolution Copernicus Sentinel data, which was initiated in the spring of 20223. This work is coordinated with Kyiv Polytechnic Institute (KPI), which has a unique capacity to generate high-quality synoptic crop maps for the entire Ukrainian territory, and the World Bank team, which implements EU-funded support programmes in land market transparency and, since the start of the Russian war against Ukraine, financial support schemes for the agricultural sector. Our joint work feeds the World Bank econometric impact analysis of the conflict and is highly relevant for assessing the reconstruction needs of the sector4. Furthermore, since Ukraine became a candidate for EU accession in July 2022, elements of this work are increasingly relevant for Ukraine's preparation for the Common Agricultural Policy. Our analysis relies on a progressive and increasingly detailed identification of crops as the growing season advances. Early season winter crop area estimation is based on detecting the NDVI values at parcel level in the period from 1November to the first cut-off date in spring (26March 2023 for this bulletin), as explained below. At this stage in the season, it is not yet possible to distinguish between the major winter crop types (winter wheat, winter barley and rapeseed). Detailed crop maps from previous years are used to limit this analysis to arable land and minimise confusion with permanent grassland. However, the Russian invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 has led to land abandonment either directly, due to military actions in the occupied areas, or indirectly, due to other factors that have impacted agricultural production. The severe drought in the spring and summer of 2022, which led to substantial abandonment in the southern oblasts, may have also had a knock-on effect into 2023. The

3) https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC127973 abandonment of arable land is expected to lead to a higher NDVI than that for bare land (due to the regrowth of abandoned crops, weed cover), which could thus contaminate our winter crop cover estimate.

The annual crop maps produced by KPI play a key role in our analysis. We can compare our winter crop estimates of previous years to the total area of winter crops in the final crop maps for the same years. Abandonment is likely to be crop-specific, for instance, more likely for unharvested summer crops. The classification accuracy of the annual crop maps is thus essential, and we have developed an independent method to cross-check and confirm the reported high accuracies. For this, we integrate country-wide parcel delineations, which have been produced by the European service company Sinergise

Ltd, with sponsorship from the European Space Agency's

Network of Resources. Sinergise uses an open-source instance segmentation technique to delineate agricultural parcels from Sentinel-2 image stacks for the period 15May to 15 July. The parcel delineations contain around 3.5million features for each year in the series

(2017-2022) representing around 30Mha of arable land.

This excludes most of the grassland and crop cultivation in small parcels, due to masking and filtering, which explains the difference with the total crop area in the KPI crop maps (around 44Mha). The major advantage in using parcel features is the possibility of analysing parcelspecific crop trends with the use of very fast grouped reduction algorithms. This is particularly useful when integrating Sentinel-1 imagery. Since the latter provides consistent and regularly sampled time series that are not affected by cloud cover, they contribute to the robustness of our analysis framework.

These categories are then split by the thresholded NDVImax to group them into potential winter crops (gold bars) or potential spring or summer crops (blue bars). Total area by grouped category is on the y-axis.

In Figure 1, we illustrate our method to cross-check abandonment. Our hypothesis is that if parcels are abandoned there should be an undisturbed trend towards increasing NDVI, without a clear bare soil transition, since that would indicate preparation for crop cultivation. For parcels sown for winter crops, field preparation should occur in the September-November period and they should then show a NDVI above our threshold (which we set at 0.25) before the cut-off date. We show analysis results for the oblasts of Mykolayivs’ka and Donets’ka, which are on opposite sides of the current conflict frontline. Results are presented for the wheat, maize, sunflower and ‘uncultivated’ crop classes in the 2022 KPI crop map. Most wheat parcels in 2022 show a clear bare soil state in September. Only less than half the area re-emerges as winter crops in 2023, with the remainder left for spring or summer crop preparations. Only a small proportion of parcels show no bare soil state in September or October, about half of which are detected as winter crops. This proportion of parcels may relate to abandonment. For maize, most of the bare soil detection is in October, and only a small proportion is prepared as winter crops. A more significant proportion of maize parcels show no bare soil state, which is likely due to late harvesting. The trends in both oblasts are more or less equal for both wheat and maize. They differ more significantly for sunflower, which shows a higher conversion to winter crops in Mykolayivs’ka than in Donets’ka. This may be related to the more severe drought impact on sunflower in Mykolayivs’ka. An interesting trend is visible in the ‘uncultivated’ crop class of 2022: the majority of parcels exhibit a bare soil state and a significant share emerges as winter crops in 2023.

The small ‘none’ category in this case suggests that permanent abandonment is not particularly evident. The overall conversion to winter crops is significantly higher in

Mykolayivs’ka than in Donets’ka.

Figure 2. Winter crop cover estimate on 26March 2023 vs winter crop occurrence in 2022 (from KPI crop map) aggregated at village council level. Areas with <10% winter crop cover in 2022 are masked.

Figure 2 shows our estimate of trends in winter crop area based on the observations until 26March 2023 compared with their reported occurrence in 2022. Agrometeorological conditions in spring 2023 are comparable to those of 2021, with winter crops that are advanced in development in comparison to the spring of 2022 (see main bulletin). A direct comparison of estimates derived at equivalent growing degree days (GDD) and regressed against the official 2021 figures shows an area decrease in 2023 (8.0 Mha) with respect to both 2021 (9.0 Mha) and 2022 (8.8 Mha). These estimates are for the integral Ukrainian territory, including the occupied areas.

The estimates in the occupied oblasts are 1.8 Mha in 2023 vs 2.3 Mha in 2021, i.e. a drop of 22%. The significant drop in winter crops in the occupied areas needs to be verified with area estimates for spring and summer crops later this season to determine whether it signals a significant increase in abandonment. We stress that the cut-off date of 26 March 2023 currently used is very early in the season and that a more accurate winter crop estimate should preferably include observations until 15 April

2023.
