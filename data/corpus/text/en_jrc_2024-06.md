# JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook: Crop monitoring in Ukraine, June 2024

<!-- page 1 -->

Issued: 17 June 2024

JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

Ukraine, June 2024

## JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook Crop monitoring European neighbourhood Ukraine

## June 2024 Below average yield outlook for winter crops

While weather conditions were overall positive until April, a rain deficit in May followed by a heatwave in June adversely affected the yield potential for winter crops. The yield forecast is reduced compared to 2023 but remains close to the 5-year average. Production forecasts are below last year’s level for all grains except soybeans.

Following an overall warm winter, spring weather was marked by contrasts. A persistent rain deficit in the east led to very poor crop conditions, while favourable conditions prevailed in the west until April. In May, a significant rain deficit impacted winter crops during flowering across the country; and in June, a heatwave in the east threatened grain filling for cereals. Field abandonment continues to be observed in several parts of the country. For Ukraine as a whole, there is a decrease in the area of winter cereals compared to last year, particularly affecting oblasts close to the front line and in the northeast near the Russian border. The rapeseed area has slightly decreased compared to 2023. The areas for grain maize and sunflower remained stable, while soybean has increased.

Compared with last year, at the country level, our forecast for grain production is reduced for all crops (except for soybean) due to decreases in both area and yields. The production for wheat is forecast at 20.7 MT (down 25% compared to 2023), and for winter barley at 2.9 MT (down 12%), with the caveat that 12.5% of the production of these cereals remains in oblasts under Russian occupation. Rapeseed production is forecast to be reduced by 12% compared to 2023, mostly due to a negative yield outlook in the south of the country. Our yield forecasts for summer crops currently follow the 5-year average.

## Weather Review

Overall, warmer-than-usual conditions were observed in most of Ukraine from late autumn to spring. However, substantial temperature swings occurred during this period, which brought frequent thaws followed by cold snaps. Spring was much warmer than average, particularly the last dekad of May and early June. Drier-than-usual conditions in southern regions in October were interrupted by wetter-than-usual November and January, but reoccurred since then, particularly in southern and eastern regions. Most regions were drier-than-usual in May and early June.

## Crop growth conditions -

## Winter crops

Mixed weather conditions since the beginning of the season have resulted in suboptimal development of winter cereals, particularly in the eastern and southern oblasts. Consequently, yield potentials were reduced, and the yield forecasts at national level are below the historical trend.

The remote sensing map above reflects predominantly winter crop conditions because summer crops, for which the season just started, contribute minimally to the variation in fAPAR at this point in time. In the western and northern regions of the country, the map indicates overall above-average biomass accumulation, which is mainly due to an early onset of regrowth after the winter. However, most other parts of the country (excluding Odes’ka and Mykolayivs'ka), present a negative anomaly. The warmer-than-usual conditions at the start of the season for wintercrops, in October and November, were favorable for sowing of winter cereals. However, in the south (Odes’ka), the significantly drier-than-usual conditions resulted in poorly developed stands.

<!-- page 8 -->

The winter was significantly warmer than usual, and winter crops were not exposed to any severe cold spells. These favourable conditions led to above-average leaf area at the end of the winter in most of the country, except in the southern oblasts such as Odes’ka, where the water deficit persisted until mid-March. Since the beginning of March, continuing mild temperatures allowed winter crops to emerge from dormancy and to restart growth early, supported by regular rainfall until the end of April. Only the eastern oblasts experienced significant water deficits (from early- March to mid-April) that hampered crop growth and development. In the south (mostly, Odes’ka and Mykolayivs'ka), the abundant rain at the end of March mitigated the adverse effects of the dry winter. Cereals are growing at average to above average rates in these two oblasts, but rapeseed was severely impacted by the dry conditions during winter. This is reflected in a decrease in rapeseed detection from remote sensing (see appendix A). In May, a significant and prolonged rain deficit adversely affected the cereals during flowering and the grain filling phase. In early June, the resurgence of rainfall in the western half of the country mitigated the impacts, particularly in the north, where plant senescence had not yet commenced. Meanwhile, the eastern half of the country experienced a substantial number of consecutive hot days, with maximum temperatures exceeding 30 degrees Celsius. This heatwave which continued after the review period is expected to affect the grain filling process of cereal crops. Given the contrastful weather conditions depicted above, the yield outlook for winter crops varies from fairly positive in the west and north to strongly negative in the east. In Luhans’ka and Donets’ka, the yield forecast is close to the lowest of the past 5 years. At the national level, our forecast for the three winter crops remains below the historical trend for the government-controlled areas and the country as a whole. It is worth noting that these forecasts consider a disruption of the positive yield trend that the country experienced over the past 20 years due to agrotechnological developments. In the context of the war, we considered a pause in this trend due to the logistical and financial difficulties faced by Ukrainian farmers..

## Spring and summer crops Spring barley

In late February, the sowing campaign of spring barley started in southern Ukraine; sooner than usual thanks to mild temperatures of late winter. Sowing progress was initially hampered by frequent rains, but got to full swing after 20 March. Above-average temperatures and sparse rain supported the good pace of the sowing campaign, and by the end of April it was concluded all over Ukraine, one to two weeks earlier than usual. Warmer-than-usual temperatures in March and April resulted in above-average phenological development, leaf area expansion and biomass accumulation. Crop water supply was adequate in the western and central regions. However, in eastern parts of the country (e.g. Kharkivs’ka, Donets’ka, Luhans’ka), where rainfall was well below the LTA, the high temperatures of in June and low soil moisture contents can compromise the yield formation during the flowering and grain filling period.

## Sunflowers, grain maize and soybeans

Abundant precipitation in late March, early April somewhat hampered the field preparations for summer crops. The sowing of sunflower started in the first dekad of April, while maize and soybean sowing began just in the second dekad of April in the southernmost areas. Progress of sowing was hampered until late April by frequent and

1 https://minagro.gov.ua/map considerable rainfall events. However, reports from early May indicate a marked uptick in the pace of sowing, which persisted through the end of May. (e.g. Sums’ka). Planting efforts, particularly for soybeans, extended into June. It is anticipated that all planting will be concluded by mid-June. According to the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food, the soybean area in the government-controlled areas presents (as reported on 06 June, 2024 1) an increase compared with the 2023 crop season. April's unusually warm temperatures offered optimal conditions for sprouting, emergence and accelerated initial development of summer crops. However, the cold period of mid-May slowed down the development of early sown summer crops and delayed the emergence of those sown later. Around 10 May, frosts events occurred, mainly in the north-eastern regions, causing local damage to the seedlings of maize and sunflower, but also negatively affecting spring barley. This delay in crop development lessened towards late May and early June, thanks to higher-than-average temperatures. Overall, canopy expansion and leaf area index of summer crops are at an average level. However, the rainfall deficit in the east raises concerns regarding a possible summer drought. The yield forecast for summer crops currently follows the 5-year average, however there is a risk of negative impacts due to unfavourable dry and hot conditions during summer primarily in the eastern regions..

## Appendix Crop area and crop phenology – an analysis using Copernicus Sentinel-2 data A- Winter and spring crops area

The area for winter crops in government-controlled areas was deduced from the sowing reports delivered by the Ministry of Agriculture as of the end of December. From these reports, we obtained the sown areas for winter wheat, winter barley, and rapeseed. However, these numbers did not entirely match the press release from the Ministry dated April 172. This was particularly the case for the rapeseed area, which in the sowing report was underestimated by 20%. We adjusted the per-oblast areas for rapeseed accordingly, to be in line with the information of the more recently published press release. Using the same methodology as in last year’s June edition of the Ukraine bulletin3, we estimated the identified the areas of winter and spring crops based on the analysis of time series of Sentinel-2 imagery. This method was applied to all oblasts of Ukraine, but only the results for the occupied oblasts are reported in the Bulletin. First, we applied a spectral threshold on the NDVI maximum value composite from February to end of May to identify pixels with winter and spring crops; then, rapeseed was distinguished from these crops, using a spectral threshold on the yellow index maximum value composite show the overall match between the end-of-campaign sowing progress reports and the remote sensing based estimates for both winter and spring crops and rapeseed. In the case of rapeseed, the most distinct discrepancies are for

Odes'ka and Mykolayivs'ka. In both oblasts the reported area is much larger than the areas inferred from the satellite-based yellow index composites. We attribute this discrepancy to the dry conditions that prevailed until the end of April in these regions, which negatively affected rapeseed flowering. The areas of winter cereals were calculated by subtracting the rapeseed areas from the total areas of winter crops. These outputs were then distributed among individual crops according to the 5-year average shares for winter wheat, winter barley, and spring barley.

## B- LAI crop-specific analysis

We derived the leaf area index (LAI) profiles for winter cereal and rapeseed parcels identified using the method described in Appendix A. We then assessed the growth patterns of these crops by comparing them with parcels represented in crop maps from preceding years from the same oblast. Utilizing high-resolution Sentinel-2 imagery, this approach offers a notable improvement over the method normally employed in the Bulletin, which is based on non-crop-specific MODIS data. At the regional level, we aggregated the LAI profiles for various parcels of winter cereals and winter rapeseed. The 2024 time series ends at varying dates for different regions, due to the variability in cloud cover and the availability of Sentinel-2 observations.
