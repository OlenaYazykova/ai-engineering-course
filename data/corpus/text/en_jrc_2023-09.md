# JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook: Crop monitoring in Ukraine, September 2023

<!-- page 1 -->

JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook

Ukraine, September 2023

## JRC MARS Bulletin - Global outlook Crop monitoring European neighbourhood Ukraine September 2023 Good yield outlook for summer crops, but reduced production of grain maize

Throughout the summer of 2023, Ukraine experienced predominantly favourable weather conditions, with aboveaverage rainfall in July. In most of the country, the yield outlook for summer crops exceeds the 5-year average by more than 10%. The southern-central oblasts experienced below-average rainfall in August, but this did not cause severe damage to summer crops.

Winter and spring cereals benefited from favourable spring weather conditions. The yield forecasts for wheat and winter barley remain unchanged, i.e., above average in most of the country. Spring barley benefited from abundant rainfall during grain filling, leading to an upward revision in yield forecasts for all oblasts. The favourable conditions in July boosted the development of summer crops. Yield forecasts were revised upwards for most of the country, except for the southern and western oblasts. However, the summer crop area is significantly reduced compared with the period before Russia's invasion in Ukraine, particularly in oblasts located in the conflict zones.

Despite the forecast increase in yield by 19%, we expect that the production of grain maize at national level will be reduced by 3% compared to the 2017-2021 average. The areas cultivated with sunflowers and soybeans were less affected than maize, and our production forecast increased to 17% (sunflowers) and 30% (soybeans) above the pre-war average. We estimate that of the total production of summer crops 3% of grain maize, 17% of sunflowers, and 6% of soybeans remain in occupied territory.

## Meteorological overview

Warmer-than-usual conditions prevailed in the country, particularly in western parts and during August. A drier-thanusual start to the summer (June) was followed by a wetter-than-usual July; and then a drier-than-usual August, particularly in the second half of the month in eastern and central regions.

## June

- Drier-than-usual conditions were observed in parts of eastern Ukraine (Luhans’ka, Donets’ka) and most of central Ukraine, with rainfall only 0-50% of the long-term average (LTA) in most of Cherkas’ka, parts of Vinnyts’ka, Kirovohrads’ka and Mykolayivs’ka, and in the northern and southernmost parts of Odes’ka oblast.

- Warmer-than-usual conditions, with daily average temperatures exceeding the LTA by between 0.5 °C and 2 °C, were observed in most of Vinnyts’ka, as well as in southern Zhytomyrs’ka and parts of Cherkas’ka, Kirovohrads’ka, Mikolayivs’ka and Odes’ka oblasts. Colder-than-usual conditions, with daily average temperatures down to between 0.5 °C and 2 °C below the LTA, were observed in eastern Ukraine and in parts of L’vivs’ka, Ivano-Frankivs’ka and Zakarpats’ka oblasts in the west.

## July

- Wetter-than-usual conditions were observed in most of the country, with rainfall exceeding the LTA by 100% and more in central (Kyyvs’ka, Cherkas’ka), eastern (Kharkivs’ka, Dnipropetrovs’ka, Zaporiz’ka) and southern

<!-- page 3 -->

(Mikolayivs’ka, Odes’ka, Krym) Ukraine. Drier-than-usual conditions prevailed in eastern Ukraine (Luhans’ka and Donets’ka).

- Warmer-than-usual conditions prevailed in most of western and southern Ukraine. Colder-than-usual conditions were observed in parts of northern (Chernihivs’ka, Sums’ka, Kyyivs’ka, Poltavs’ka) and eastern (Donets’ka) regions of the country.

## August

- Drier-than-usual conditions were observed in eastern Ukraine (parts of Luhans’ka, Donets’ka, most of Zaporiz’ka and parts of Khersons’ka and Krym oblasts) and in central Ukraine (Zhytomyrs’ka, Kyyivs’ka, southern Chernihivs’ka, Cherkas’ka, Vinnyts’ka and parts of Khmel’nyts’ka and Odes’ka oblasts), and extended across most of the country in the second half of August. Wetter-than-usual conditions, with rainfall exceeding the LTA by more than 100%, were observed in northern central Ukraine (Sums’ka, Poltavs’ka) in the first half of August.

- Warmer-than-usual conditions were observed across the country, with average daily temperatures exceeding the LTA by between 0.5 °C and 2 °C in most of eastern Ukraine, and up to 4 °C in western Ukraine.

## Crop growth conditions Winter and spring crops

Favourable spring weather conditions boosted the development of winter and spring crops, with June providing ideal conditions for growth. However, heavy rainfall in July delayed harvesting and potentially affected grain quality, while dry conditions in August hindered rapeseed sowing.

Winter and spring crops benefited from favourable spring conditions, with overall mild temperatures that boosted their development. Late spring was drier than usual in the north-western half of Ukraine, which was beneficial during the flowering stage. Weather conditions in June allowed steady growth and development of winter crops through the grain-filling stage, without them experiencing water stress or temperature-related stress. As phenological development followed a seasonal course, harvesting of winter crops started in late June / early July. However, substantial rainfall between 6 and 9 July in a large part of the country (to the west of a band expanding from Vinnyts'ka and Odes’ka to Chernihivs'ka and Kharkivs'ka) delayed the start or impeded the progress of harvesting. Later in July, frequent rains additionally delayed the progress of harvesting (by a week at national level) compared to 2022. Dry weather conditions in August allowed the work to catch up and harvesting was completed in late August. The wet conditions in July may have had an adverse impact on grain quality, which remains to be confirmed. The yield forecasts for rapeseed, winter wheat and winter barley remain unchanged from the June edition of the Bulletin, i.e. above the trend throughout the country except in north-eastern parts (Kharkivs'ka). Spring barley, which is cultivated more in the north-east, benefited from abundant rainfall during grain filling, and the yield forecasts were revised upwards for all oblasts. The dry conditions in most of the country starting in mid- August caused topsoils to become very dry, resulting in delays to the rapeseed sowing campaign. It is most likely that the 10-40 mm of rainfall in late August and early September, in the eastern half of the country, has been sufficient for favourable emergence conditions; however, more rain would be beneficial.

## Summer crops

Differences between the Normalised Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) cumulated from 1 to 31 August 2023 and the medium-term average (MTA, 2013-2022) for the same periods, in arable land areas.

Summer crop sowings were delayed compared to the previous season, due to abundant spring rainfall. Higherthan-usual temperatures, which persisted until mid-June, accelerated crop development. Maize and sunflowers benefited from the overall wetter-than-usual summer conditions, despite occasional rainfall deficits in June and August in southern regions. High yields are expected in major production areas.

Grain maize, mostly cultivated in the central and northernmost parts of Ukraine, benefited from aboveaverage precipitation from June to August. Most fields did not experience water stress during the season. In June and July, temperatures were generally in line with the LTA (slightly below average in the eastern part of the country), which was beneficial for vegetative development and flowering. In August, rainfed crops in areas that were affected by high temperatures combined with rainfall deficits (such as in Vinnyts’ka, Zhytomyrs’ka and Kyyvs’ka), presented an early onset of ripening, thus reducing the potential for very high yields under rainfed cultivation.

Sunflower production is concentrated in the eastern and south-eastern regions of the country, which experienced no long dry period until mid-August. This was particularly true in Dnipropetrovs'ka, where rainfall exceeded the LTA by 15% during the 3-month period. Starting in mid- August, a significant rain deficit caused an early onset of leaf senescence. Additionally, the ongoing war in the frontline regions has led to a significant reduction in cultivated areas. It is worth noting that the strongly positive anomaly observed in the remote sensing map for August in some areas (Khersons'ka, Zaporiz’ka, Donets’ka, Kharkivs’ka) is closely related to spontaneous vegetation growth associated with the increasing abandonment of land due to the current and past effects of the war (see Appendix 2). Abandoned land tends to exhibit late starts in canopy development in spring (due to lack of management, as reported in the March Bulletin) and high biomass during summer due to favourable weather conditions, in particular in areas which in a normal season have a high share of ripening and harvested winter crops around this time.

<!-- page 7 -->

Soybeans are primarily grown in the western half of Ukraine. The oblasts of Khmel’nyts’ka, Zhytomyrs’ka and Vinnyts’ka experienced average rainfall, temperature and radiation levels, which were favourable for soybean cultivation. However, starting in the second dekad of August, these regions observed 2 weeks with less than 5 mm of rainfall, leading to crop senescence and reduced grain filling, and diminishing the high yield potential.

At the national level, our yield forecast for the three summer crops exceeds historical trends, with increases above the 5-year average for grain maize (+20%), sunflowers (+20%), and soybean (+7%). This translates to respectively 29%, 12%, and 7% increase in yields compared to the yields observed in 2022.

## Appendix Appendix 1: Summer crop area estimates

Figure 1. Comparison of KPI remote sensing based areas estimate of summer crops as compared to Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food (MAPF) of Ukraine for 2023 (left) and 2021 (right).

The Kyiv Polytechnic Institute (KPI) map used for our estimates of the area of summer crops in nongovernment-controlled oblasts was released on 10 July 2023, and is based on Copernicus Sentinel-1 and Sentinel- 2 data up to the end of June. While the winter crop estimates demonstrate high reliability (e.g., rapeseed with an accuracy1 of 0.97), the accuracy of the estimates of summer crops areas is lower. When considered as a single class, summer crops are correctly distinguished from other crops (accuracy of 0.93).; however, the differentiation of the three major summer crops (grain maize, sunflower, and soybean) is not sufficiently accurate (accuracies ranging from 0.62 to 0.83) to serve as a basis for area monitoring. The figure 1-left presents the 2023 KPI summer crop estimates for government-controlled oblasts in comparison to the figures provided by the Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food (MAPF) of Ukraine. It demonstrates an overall good match. However, two oblasts, Dnipropetrovs'ka and Kharkivs'ka, distinctly

1 Accuracy refers to F1-score.

deviate from the main regression line. These two oblasts, located near the current or previous conflict zone, contain a significant amount of non-cultivated areas (see Appendix 2). A similar comparative analysis using the 2021 KPI map, conducted on consolidated official statistics, reveals an excellent correlation with minimal bias (figure 1-right). This reinforces the trust in the high quality of the 2023 KPI map for assessing summer crop areas. For the four occupied oblasts (Luhans'ka, Donets'ka, Khersons'ka, Zaporiz'ka), we utilized the 2023 KPI estimates of summer crops, which were unbiased based on the regression calculated using other oblasts except for Dnipropetrovs'ka and Kharkivs'ka. Since the method was found to be unsatisfactory for the detection of individual summer crops, we divided the summer crop areas into grain maize, sunflower, and soybean classes using the average shares from official statistics for the years 2017- 2021..

## Appendix 2: Detection of abandoned land

Figure 2. Percentage of abandoned land at village level in 2023 with respect to arable land in 2021 (source: Kyiv

Polytechnic Institute).

Based on evidence from high-resolution Copernicus Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 imagery, up to 4.7 Mha of agricultural land has been abandoned in 2023 (source: Kyiv Polytechnic Institute). Abandoned land is mostly concentrated along the current and past conflict zone. Because of sufficient rainfall, abandoned land appears as fully vegetated due to extensive weed cover. Abandonment is mostly attributed to inaccessibility and the extensive presence of mines and unexploded ordnance. Note that the conflict area is also marked with high values in the MODIS NDVI mosaic (see section on crop canopy conditions). The impact of the Kakhovka dam break on 6 June 2023 is clearly reflected in abandonment of areas that were flooded downstream in south-western Khersons’ka oblast. A somewhat surprising effect is visible on the Crimean Peninsula, which is currently not affected by large-scale military activities. The abandonment here is likely triggered by the lack of irrigation water linked to the destruction of the Kakhovka dam.

## Appendix 3: Comparison of wheat area, production and yield 2023 forecasts for government-controlled areas

Figure 3. The three graphs at the top show the 2023 forecasts for production (left), yield (centre) and area (right) of wheat in the government-controlled areas in Ukraine, according to assessments by different entities at several dates during the season. The three graphs at the bottom show the actual amount of wheat harvested (left), the average yield of the area harvested (centre) and the area harvested (right) during the course of the wheat harvesting campaign, as reported by the Ukrainian Ministry of Agrarian Policy and Food (MAPF). The final values of the 2023 MAPF harvest reports are shown as dotted horizontal lines in the graphs at the top.

In the earliest assessments, most distinctly until May, the JRC MARS production and yield forecasts clearly stand out by being substantially higher than the forecasts from the other entities, but well in line with the final figures. According to JRC MARS, the high forecasts are based on predominantly favourable weather conditions in the important wheat production areas in the country, and fairto-good crop conditions as inferred from remote sensing imagery. These observations suggest that, despite the constraints imposed by the war, overall crop management can be expected to be adequate in most of the areas sown to wheat. The other sources (e.g. Agritel) confirmed the overall favourable weather and crop conditions, but assumed a strong negative effect of the ongoing war on final yields and production.

In June, based on continued favourable conditions, JRC MARS revised its yield forecast further up, but revised the area estimate downwards, mainly due to a much higherthan-usual share of rapeseed, which could be distinguished from the other winter crops during flowering, and KPI reports on extensive land abandonment (see also Appendix 2). This resulted in a moderate increase in the JRC MARS production forecast. The majority of the other entities sharply increased their production estimates only after the first actual harvesting figures were published in July by the Ukrainian MAPF2. NASA Harvest is one of the other entities known to integrate high resolution Earth observation in their analysis, resulting in close agreement with the final figures, but only at the onset of harvest.
