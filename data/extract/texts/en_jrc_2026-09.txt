JRC MARS Bulletin – Global outlook
Crop monitoring in Ukraine – September 2026
Mixed weather conditions prevailed during the summer, leading to a contrasting yield outlook for summer
crops. Persistent drought in the west and rainfall deficits in parts of the centre resulted in below-average
yields, while fair to positive yields are expected elsewhere, supported by timely water supply and the
absence of severe thermal stress.
For winter crops, the generally drier- and warmer-than-usual weather in June did not significantly change
crop conditions, matching the overall positive yield outlook reported in the June edition of this bulletin.
However, rain in July delayed harvesting and deteriorated crop quality.
Following a delayed sowing campaign for summer crops due to locally dry and cold weather in spring, a
reduction in soybean area was observed. In the west and parts of the centre, crops delayed in development
faced rainfall shortages together with above-average temperatures, resulting in suboptimal vegetative
growth. Elsewhere, crop conditions remained fair to positive, mainly due to abundant rainfall in July. The
impact of rain deficit in August is expected to be limited.
Considering the spatial distribution of production areas, slightly above-average yields are forecast for grain
maize and sunflower, while, for soybean, mainly grown in the west, yields are expected to be below
average at the country level. Because of this and the slightly increased areas of grain maize and sunflower,
the production for both grain maize and sunflower is expected to be fair, while soybean production is
expected to fall well below the five-year average.

1. Weather review (1 June – 10 September 2026)
Summer was characterised by marked regional variability in temperature, with warmer-than-average
conditions in the west and cooler conditions concentrated in the east, particularly in July. Rainfall was
generally below average in June, especially in the east, while July was notably wetter than usual across
eastern regions. August was drier than usual, again across most regions.
1.1 Summer (1 June–10 September)
June
• Slightly warmer-than-average conditions were
observed only in the west and parts of the south,
including parts of Mykolayivs’ka and
neighbouring areas in Odes’ka, with daily
average temperatures up to 2 °C above the
long-term average (LTA) in general, reaching up
to 3 °C above the LTA mainly in L’vivs’ka.
Elsewhere, temperatures remained about
average.
• Distinctly drier-than-usual conditions affected
the east and westernmost areas, with
precipitation about 50–70 % below average.
Conditions were slightly wetter than usual
mainly in Zhytomyrs’ka and neighbouring regions
in northern Kyyivs’ka and western Chernihivs’ka,
and also locally in the south (parts of southern
Vinnyts’ka and northernmost and southernmost
Odes’ka).

July
• Slightly colder-than-usual conditions prevailed
across most regions, with average daily
temperatures as much as 2 °C below the LTA,
most prominently in the east. In north-eastern
Poltavs’ka and neighbouring areas in western
Kharkivs’ka, average daily temperatures reached
3 °C below the LTA. Near-average temperatures
prevailed elsewhere.
• Wetter-than-usual conditions were observed in
most of the east, except from central
Kharkivs’ka southwards to Zaporiz’ka. In the
wetter regions, total precipitation exceeded the
LTA by up to 150 % or more. Drier-than-usual
conditions affected parts of the central and
western regions, where total precipitation was
below average; the southernmost areas of these
regions received only 50–70 % of the usual
precipitation.
